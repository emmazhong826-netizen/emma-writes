Work Log 03: Eleven Cafes

2026. 10. 2

Morning — woke up at 10am, dilly-dallying until 12pm, which includes listening to random news, scrolling Rednote posts, exercising for thirty minutes, and uncommonly, making up. This is to prepare for the forthcoming visits to the coffee shops, which is part of Ling Duo’s marketing campaigns. From 1pm-6pm, I reached out to 11 local cafes and bookshops. I consider it a gain because of the good first impressions I made on all the shop owners. There are opportunities for sustained collaboration whatsoever the result is for the upcoming LDN promotion. [I was a killer in the business of first impression, and I knew it.]

I was exhausted after the visits, as I noted earlier:

“It was a genuinely tiring day. I was internally depleted, my body is light and indefensible like sand to the vacuum. At 7pm, I still made my way to the English corner, knowing that social interaction is an overload to me. I’m already here anyway, accidentally 30 minutes earlier than the event started which is why I had the time to write now. I kind of regret it. My flight is tomorrow and I haven’t started packing yet. Fuck it. I’m already here.”

It’s all done now. I came back and the old, stubborn habit of rumination found me. Dear, I contemplated, again and again, if I had done all of the social gestures appropriately. It’s not constructive—the more I thought about it, the more I’d be prone to escapism. So, thanks to my self-awareness, I stopped. I checked my outlooks, and I danced in front of the mirror.

The two people who sat by my side during the English corner are two “sharks”. The description may not be accurate, but I can’t find the apt word. It tries to connote a sense of danger—as if they can devour me and my innocence at any second if I don’t guard the latter properly.

The woman on my left spoke very openly about beauty, family, and the kinds of choices she had made in arranging her life—what news! The one on the right was so “heartless”. She was so awashed in the transactional, profited-driven culture. I can see a gain-oriented, selfish personality forming, coming with which, an unsalvageable humanity.

I don’t apologize for my observation. I don’t really care, and I felt somehow hardened by my own exhaustion too. There are a few things left — shower, packing, cleaning, and a sound sleep—before I see the morning sun and kiss goodbye to Guangzhou.
