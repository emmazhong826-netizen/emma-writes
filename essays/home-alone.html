杂记：Home Alone

2026. 7. 13

I have not been updating on life for a little while. I may have lost my voice in the past days. I was preoccupied, by a few things at the same time. Hugo and I had agreed to remain friends, in the prospect of an unfruitful, long-distance romance. Our love has no roots, our love has no fruits, our love is a summer Mayflower. He plunged back to his Russian research in Vilnius, Lithuania. I graduated and came back to China, with the tentative plan to work for at least one year before deciding to accumulate more degrees or not, in Canada, or elsewhere.

It has been difficult to integrate the relationship. Unhappiness and isolation found me unexceptionally. Looking back, I have easily allowed myself to take on his sorrows as my own, but neither expected nor prepared for the devouring darkness of his past. Thus, I ruminated on estalishing boundaries in romantic relationships.

It feels surreal to come back home after seven years of wandering in a foreign country. What is foreign and familiar has inverted.

It is in the triviality—in the subconscious moves to eat, walk, and banter—that I realized my aloneness. The brutal reality is, I no longer belong here. I don't belong 'over there' either. Home is where your heart and anchors are, Jeff shares. En effet. It can only be so now.

But I envy the rooted people, the ones that stayed. They have never left home, and perhaps will never. There is an unthinking seamlessness and buoyancy in their disposition and interaction in the local theatre. I'm both previledged and exiled. I'm now afflicted with the contraditions of a more bare world, and I can't unsee it. I'm not alone though; I have seen loners. Enlightened and composed, contemptuous or insecure, they are at all times defaulted by a deep-seated reservation, if not difficulty, to identify with most crowds. I presume so for the case of Hugo too—books are his mind's last citadel.

I have attempted many means to sooth my unsettlness, the need to find new refuge at my own home. I remained close contacts with friends, exercised a kind of running that pushes my body beyond my mind, and have not allowed my endearing books to leave my hands. This is also coupled by flowing pages of Socratic reflection I secretly enjoy in my solitude. I may say now, I have rested a little and starting to taste a beginning of something new, a life that is forming again.
