杂记：TRUST

日期未记

I am managing life.

    Or at least, I am trying.

          I came home from a bustling restaurant and from the company of a friend. Something between us felt as if it were hardening into a wall, and I was no longer sure whether the wall could still be broken.

          I went home and entered the darker place inside my own heart.

          I manage my tears. I manage my emotions. I manage my imagination.

          Without management, everything seems ready to run wild like an unbridled horse rushing toward the edge of a bridge.

          That evening, I felt that my friend did not see me as I was.

          Under her gaze, or perhaps under my imagination of her gaze, I became smaller than I really was.

          I felt uncomfortable showing vulnerability.

          I felt unalive.

          I wanted to grow up, of course. I wanted to see things as they were, like a woman rather than a frightened girl.

          But people move through the world differently.

          Some people move with their blades showing: sharp, shining, almost dazzling.

          I move more like a budding flower: unobtrusive, fluid, intimate to whoever is willing to look closely.

          Perhaps I belittled myself.

          Perhaps she also did not see me clearly.

          But what could I really expect?

          Understanding may be one of the most luxurious things in the world.

          How can I expect another person, even someone close to me, to understand me exactly as I understand myself?

          Misunderstanding is part of life.

          Loneliness is too.

          Even when it is unbearable.

          As I write this, I miss my family.

          My eyes fill again.

          It is not always easy to occupy a room entirely by myself, even with soft music playing in the background.

          But I can manage this moment.

          Life teaches in its own way.

          I can adapt.

          I can study.

          I can work.

          And I can learn to trust.

          The question is:

          If other people do not see me as I am, do I stop being myself?

          No.

          I can change the way I communicate.

          I can compromise where compromise is useful.

          But I do not need to compromise everything.

          I have failed people before.

          That does not mean I am a failure.

          I write these words with as much certainty as I can gather:

          Emma, I trust you.
