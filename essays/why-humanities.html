SOCIAL COMMENTARY

社会评论：我们为什么还需要人文学科

2024. 12. 18

Preface:

To the contemporaries (those of us in the 20s seeking a career), there are something I’d like to share since the future remains uncertain, and wavers in the air for all of us. Increasingly, I feel we are ‘beasts of burden’, needing a constant load at our back — we have to remain ‘busy’. We are either socializing, video gaming, obssessively racing i.e., in the career rum, or in front of the screen. We need to get better at being alone, doing nothing but be with our hearts.

In the article, you'll discover:

- Why do we feel lost, confused, hollow inside, is life truly meaningless?

- Arts vs Science explained: What is the use of studying liberal arts at school?

- The importance of art, literature, and culture for contemprary societies.

- As Arts students, how to integrate our skills to contemporary society?

Listening to Jordan Peterson earlier,

I was touched by his words,“the meaning of life is proportionate to the responsibility that we take on”. Some of us feel rootless, directionless, and hollow-hearted perhaps because we are too ‘atomized’ to remember that we are part of a historical tradition, a culture, and of a family. Our ancestors made possible of our human life and every facets of our modern enjoyments—culture, technology, and education—all of which we might have been taken for granted for too long, blissfully and ignorantly.

Referencing Edmund Burke, all of us are involuntarily tasked in a “sacred covenant” between the dead, the living, and the yet unborn. We are indebted to the culture-creators before our arrival, and are responsible for the children yet to come, at birth. Because we are alive—and, are the privileged youngsters with vigor—we need to take on the responsibility of our life in gearing it towards contextual, positive influence to our family and culture. To put it simply, no man is an island, we are a part of a greater whole, meaningfully and beautifully.

My skillsets are shaped by my four-year study as a liberal arts student at Vancouver Institute for Higher Learning and University of British Columbia. This education is a generalist training that yields my ability to systematically (and argubly critically) think, learn, orate, and write. Ultimately, "art and literature" are conveyors, the midwife of a particular kind of “truth”—one that is distinct from scientific empiricism. The truths of unveiled by the fields in Humanities are about our fundamental human condition: our human experiences, emotions, love, meaning, psychology, and so forth.

These truth are empirical in the sense that they are real (love as an experience is real beyond the explanation of chemistry!). However, while "science" is the study of what can be articulated (i.e., the quantifiable) about the external world and ourselves. The truths in humanities inherently eludes languages. Therefore, at best, art are attempts to articulate what fundamentally can not be articulated, the knowledge of the heart. These articulation/media take forms in write-ups, poetry, performative art, photography, and so on and on. These knowledge are shared both in "conceptual" dimension (abstraction that is comprehensible by rationality, logic, intellects); and, If the producer is adroit, the art work becomes a "visceral" conduit for intricate knowledge (felt wisdoms about our existence that are embodied, can be heart-touching and "goose-bumpy").

These knowledge, what is sometimes referred to as "high culture" are necessary because they attribute meaning to life, add up layers to our perception, and enlighten the heart to appreciate beauty in the ordinary. The quality of our subject experience, for example, that of "the moment" you read through the lines of the article, are informed by your hearts, its tranquility harmonized by a subconscious aesthetics training from your past.

Our society grows deficient of culture as we are forged into consumers reading advertisement but not literature, as the young people no longer aspire to be "culture-creators" but managerial middle-class bureaucrats. We put faith in technology in solving the most pressing human bot problem of climate change and impending (possibly nuclear) warfare. On the side, we either entertain ourselves to death in digital vulgarity and pornography, or victimizing ourselves into depression lacking the systematic lesson in our education that no men is exempted from setbacks, tragedy, and illness — it is how we cope with them that matters. Money becomes the homogenizing force that overtake our faith in our human capability to make changes, culture, and love (stupid! You believe in love!). As David Brook said, one can be (and according to established correlation, more likely to be) "ultra-successful" (i.e., rich and powerful by our social definition), and emotional devoidant, spiritually bankrupted, and morally compromised—In a word, misrable in his B Side of life. Proper production and dissemination of "Art and Literature", the teachings of emotionality, meaning, and love at highest form, can help heal some but not all of our social problems. Set aside the social, they can help cope quite a lot of our individual problems.

I then wondered how can I integrate my good intention, and the skills in the framework of the society? From a first-person perspective as I clear my thoughts,

First, I need to amass the delicate gems in the humanities, a venture I can too easily expend my lifetime. These are years of unseen, "underground" work of solitude that informs character, thoughts, and creativity. I dedicated today to study (like numerous other days in the past, and many forseeable days in the future), not conversing with anyone—I guess other than you, my virtual authences. For certain, amassment of knowledge is a conversation not only with books, but genuine human beings and nature.

Second, I need to have the artistic skills of production—technical proficiency in any areas such as writing, poetry, music, art, photography, media production, research, social studies, etc.—something I'm good at, interested in, can be paid of, and the world needs (here I'm referencing "Ikigai", Japanese framework for constructing life purpose). These skills are secondary, and constitutes tools for self-expression (sure, my individuality—but non-egoistically, to help synthesize a useful system of thoughts for others, to preserve a cultural root, to unpack a history of human greatness or wretchedness). Camus said in the Plague, the greatest pain in the world is the inability for self-expression. Of course, coupled with this processes are growth in terms of our social sensibility, emotional intelligence, the necessaries for our navigation in society.

Lastly, I need to have a sensitivity and sensibility on the aesthetics of our time—the zeitgeist, as Hegal calls it. I'll need to share the knowledge I possess, deposit the wisdoms in ways that are resonating, relevant (i.e., comprehensible) to people. Truth is great because it is beautiful, but I wonder what is the purpose, or point of truth if it can't be shared with our loved ones?

Four years of university is a blink of eye. In a dream-like second, with some regrets and forgiveness, unsureness and new-determination, faith and hope, I come nearly to this intersection of life, so do you. Future unfolds in front of us, all that we can do is act and see.
