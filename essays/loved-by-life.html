散文：被爱照亮过的人

2026. 8. 8

My phone had been alarming me about its storage.

I spent the whole morning transferring photos from the past two years onto my hard drive. It was a toiling task, but it did miraculous work to my brain.

I came to see how differently I laughed in the past.

I realized how much Life and love had shined upon me. They are sufficient, as Dostoevsky stated in White Nights, to warm my heart for the rest of my lifetime.

Afterwards, I was bored with the idea of flipping through pages in a coffee shop with AC, as my routine suggested. I might have slept too late and stared at too many screens these days; my eyes were screaming in pain the night before.

I also felt some kind of need to dispense myself.

I had been so intellectually driven in the past days and weeks that I was walking on a thin connecting bridge with reality.

So I went to do my nails and lashes.

I chatted casually, didn’t think too much, and enjoyed the beauty of the “artwork”.

The woman who helped to do my nails suggested that my choice was picked less often by Chinese girls, but more by “foreigners”, specifically girls from Brazil and America.

I know.

It’s flashy and idiosyncratic.

I’m not assimilated into the conventional feminine image pursued by Chinese women. I’m not even borderline elegance in my personality.

That’s why I picked guitar and not piano in high school.

Part of my heart is always in exile, finding outlet in folklores and ballads.

Internally, I have a self-construal that is wild with grace, and bad-ass with virtue.

Later, I heard a lyrical line from Zhang Shaohan’s version of 《阿刁》 on my way back from my run:

“孤独是一种信仰。”

Then mom called to pick me up for dinner.

We arrived at our grandparents’.

Grandma cooked duck; potatoes, green peppers and eggplants; and lettuce, which I had explicitly asked for the last time.

Of course it would be on the table.

To ensure that I had the freshest bite, Grandma had prepared everything and only started cooking the lettuce the second we stepped through the door.

By the time we washed our hands, exchanged greetings, settled on the sofa, and casually ingested a few grapes, the freshest and most delicious lettuce in the world was born—

infused with the most profound intergenerational love you can ever ask for.

Moments like these touch me, and I must note them down before stress attempts to override them.

After dinner, I watched a few rounds of the Ping Pong match to let the food settle before I went for my run.

The match was between Miwa Harimoto and Shin Yu-bin.

Miwa is a rising star. I can see that.

She has a killer’s instincts in her eyes. She is patient and humble.

She is a warrior, just like Kobe is a warrior, MJ is a warrior.

I wondered about myself, and whether I can parallel them in my own domain in life.

Connecting back to my nostalgia earlier in the day, I realized that I had been imbued with so much love in life that it actually leaves no excuse for me to be trapped in a victimized narrative, even if I don’t have a perfect family.

Nobody does.

This determination elevated me so much that I was in a constant euphoric state during my run.

I rode the e-bike like the wind, superseding all of the passers-by with utmost confidence and composure.

Then I ran the first mile in only 5 minutes and 30 seconds and nearly threw up.

That was enough euphoria for one evening.

I slowed down.

And continued my run.
