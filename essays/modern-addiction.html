Deteriorating Modern Addiction

2025. 4. 16

Finished watching Addiction, an informative documentary on Opioid Overdose Epidemic in the United States, I thought of saying a few words on modern addiction, its causes, and cures. This is an Age where everyone is addicted. We use addiction to escape from something—the unbearable feeling of anxiety, depression, and our inner empitiness. The escapism can only find a host too easily—be it alcohol, tobacco, drugs, screen, pornography, or processed food. But why do we run away from our life? What exactly, are we running away from?

Conceivably, the normative structure of modern life is anxiety inducing. We live in a tight morbid cell in the city, face the screen for most of our waking life, and lived according to an artifacial clock. This is a lifestyle that deprives humans of our basic needs necessary for physical and mental health—our needs for nature, creativity, meaning, trust, and genuine human connection. Conversely, we find ourselves living in an atomized, self-interested, transactional social environment of constant needs to guard our survival against predatory co-workers await to take your advantages. How can a cog in a bureauratic economic machine be truly happy? Since no cogs can bear psychological blow of the conclusion that he has failed in life, the cog rationalizes to himself the benefits of the cog life at a conscious level. He can only passify the misery manifesting at a subconscious level through escapist means.

Moreover, the world seems to be going through a downturn. It is marked by economic volatility, geopolitical instability with Trump’s ascendency and his unpredictable foreign policy, the rise of artificial intelligence and the prospect of its substitution for human work, the increased divorce rates and fracture of family structure spreading from the North American societies to the world, and so forth. We are living a VUCA (vulnerable, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous) world—as political scientists often decribes—and this exacts a mental toll to the world citizens who lend an ear to politics. Expecting economic restructure and rejuvenation of some kind, the U.S. citizens are disappointingly awashed by of political corruption, increasing polarization, self-destruction of state appratus by the administration, and the seemingly doomed decline of the empire. To counter the worries, the decision-makers of the U.S. society have decide to "numb" its citizens from the pains, because it is the most profitable. They had been feeding the American people artifacial dopamines packaged in mass distraction—no different from the Brave New World Soma—since the 1970s. For the past half century, American people learned how to un-educate themselves and "entertaining [them]selves to death", as Neil Postman nails it. In the 1960s, the society is still capable of "revolting the system" through counter-culture movements. Today, the "morphines" are injected and entrenched in the bones of the Gen-Z generation. Political apathy, addiction, and obedience (characterized by careerist ambition to climb up in the system) are the signitures of our epochal spirit. We no longer ruminate, let along fight for the collective good. We learn to alleviate our psychological pain and emptiness through an individualized, addictive, escapist pathway. Sunken deep into the conviction that we are personal failures, we no longer ask questions. While all of these are taking place, the corporate oligarchies are also milking the last drop of our precious life productivity, concentration, health, and wallet out of our autonomous control.

What is social media addiction?

Counter-intuitively, escapism is indeed an invite (or product) for us to dissolve some unpleasant early childhood memories, sometimes trauma. For me (and many children raised in Chinese households), it is to understand and reconcile the experience of the emotional neglect from parents and the conditioning of the educational system to pursue external validation. Our early experiences (and especially our subconscious coping mechanisms to these experiences) keep wrecking havoc and forcefully dictate our behaviors to undesirable outcomes until it is tamed. I had carried an incessant, unsettling "internal critic" with myself for years—it inflicts shame, anxiety, and reinforce the mantra to me that “I am not good enough”. The only way out is confrontion with these negative emotions and tackling it in its source. But the availability of modern distraction allows people to escape and delay (sometimes never face) such confrontation—which is also processes of adulting and attainment of psychological independence. There is an unspoken cost to delayed adulthood though. Un-resolved emotional residual will stay with a person, accumulate, and carry over to next life stages, so will the identity crises. The chaos in the body can also blow in self- or other- destructive forms. The sunken cost of further attempts of confrontation also rises high as we age.

The addicts—that is you and me at a certain time of our life—feel demoralized and conceive ourselves as a personal failure. But I think we should as the more proper, and critical question: Why are these sinister addictive forces permited to enter our living sphere i.e., the mundane life of the teenagers and citizens in the first place?

When heroin become a legal pain killer readily available for purchase; when "every 25 minutes, a baby born in the United States is dependent on Opioids [because of parents drug use]"(Addiction) It becomes clear, society failed us to create a healthy environment of growth for the future generation. This means you should stop the moral blame on an individual level. The question to be asked is, how can we move forward from here?

I share some insights from the documentary. We'll need to understand the danger in our immediate environment, the available drugs and their workings:

Often pressured by Big Pharma, doctors frequently prescribes Morphine as painkillers.

Drug cartels sells:

Heroin.

Fentanyl (initially intended for anesthesia) → 50x stronger than heroin and is often mixted with common drugs.

Xanax → mild tranquilizer

Cures for overdose:

Methadone, a long-lasting opioid that can curb craving without causing euphoria.

Suboxone [← brand name] (i.e., Buprenorphine) similarly reduces the level of drug euphoria to curb addiction, less stronger than methadone.

Narcan [← brand name] can reverse overdose and pull the opiods off the brain receptors.

This is the neurobiological changes taking place in an addicted brain:

The drug use can lead to ten-fold increase in the dopamine levels in the brain → producing memory of euphoria & alter the cell connection in the brain → triggering fierce craving for the drug and induce pleasure-seeking behaviors. If not satisfied, the addict will suffer from withdrawal.

During withdrawal, the stress hormones will release at catastrophic level → leading to symptoms including shaking, pain, anxiety, and intense dysphoria → The addict is consumed by the cravings, seeking the drug (addictive source) at all cost.

Long-term addiction lead to neurological change:

The addict experiences a reduced level of dopamine d2 receptors in the brain. This means the level of dopamine produced by addict is lower than an average brain. Since dopamine is essential for motivation, people of severe addiction may also find it hard to get up in the morning because of a lack of motivation in the natural state. "Tolerance" → the addict increasingly consume not to feel high, but not to feel sick.

They also suffer from reduction in the level of gray matter in the brain Prefrontal Cortex that is essential for decision-making and behavioral control.

Addiction also alters DNA expression that open and close certain genes of the addict (e.g., impacting transmission of glutamate, etc.)

Addiction is a complex disorder, a product of the interplay of various genes and environmental factors. There is always a hope of treatment for addictions. The dopamine receptors that are reduced by addictive drugs can come back with recovery, so is the brain gray matter, as has been proven by science.

In the urban center when the convenience of the world resides by our finger tip, I hope to remind you of our human frailty and impotence in the natural world. We are manipulable, pleasure-seeking, rat-like simpletons similarly crazy for intermittent conditioning that media corporations exacted to our human life. We are left with nothing but our gut instincts and each other at the end of our day. We may think ourselves as God-like with technology, but in Antigone, Sophocles humbly reminds us, “Nothing vast enters the life of mortals without a curse." Technological convenience and short-cuts to pleasure through fast food, tabacco, pornography, and drugs may lead to longer toil in achiving progress in our "human growth". Stripping away the instagram-able experiences, the glamor of resume, and your daily pretention and elaborated self-branding to fit the inquisitive gaze of others. What is left? — Who are you, really? How do you feel right now, really? For how long have you asked that question to yourself?
