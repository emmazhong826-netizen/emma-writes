Finished reading Cioran’s 《在绝望之巅》.

I have to say — he is such a desperate and liberated writer.

His words are filled with desperation, indignation, and misery. One symbol that he uses most often is “fire” and “burning himself to death”, either with the external fire or that within his own heart.

He has such a unique view of morality, melancholy, depression, love, and life.

He shares an extremely Nietzschean view.

He thinks that there is no fundamental meaning to life, and that a life well lived is a life that is “bountifully felt”.

To live means not rejecting the Dionysian passion within ourselves. It means admitting the contradictions, perpetual uncertainty, and meaninglessness of life.

It is to live in the misery of these realizations, to the point where we become courageous enough to jump out of the bounds of morality, which is itself meaningless too; to the point where we become our own masters and become a God or a Devil.

It is a self-directed life that he is after, and the self-direction comes from an emancipatory misery.

The emancipation culminates in the realization of the meaninglessness of life and of our consciousness as, more often than not, a burden to life.

I don’t reject this specific notion.

Though I don’t think the realization of our contradictions condemns us into perpetual misery.

*En effet*, it is in uncertainty and time that we live.

The journey of life sounds exciting, no?

I was shaken by many of his ideas, and I think there is some sense in the need to **live life instead of survive life**.

To forget that our existence exists in history or time—something that predicates on our subjective experience in the first place.

Rather, our existence is immoral.

It is meaningless and immortal at the same time.

I didn’t see how he contradicts Fromm, who believes that we have instincts and capacities for both good and bad.

I just think that Cioran had gotten used to his isolation too much, to the point that he would never be comprehended by this external world.

I had gotten into it a little bit after my breakup.

I crave genuine connection, but there is none, to be honest.

There is no depth and coherence in the spiritual connection that people form in my immediate environment.

However, as Vince Lombardi said, we make the best out of what we have.

Maybe this is where I differ from Cioran.

He sees contradiction and burns inside it.

I see contradiction too, but I don’t think the realization of our contradictions condemns us into perpetual misery.
